Reinforcement Learning for Cube Reorientation and Continuous Rotation with a Simulated Dexterous Hand

Turning a cube to a target orientation, holding it there, and continuing to rotate it in the palm require different changes in contact. We studied these behaviors with a simulated ORCA Hand after assembling and calibrating its physical counterpart. An initial proximal policy optimization (PPO) policy briefly reached a +45° target in four of 128 trials and sustained it in none. Frame-aligned replays motivated controlled retraining experiments on reward, termination, available motion, and object retention. For X-axis reorientation, a selected dwell-reward policy held the target throughout the final five seconds in 187 of 192 new starts, compared with 39 for its matched reset control. For a world-Y target, allowing forearm rotation improved hand-supported terminal holds from 0 to 252 of 576 trials. For continuous Z rotation, we fixed the wrist and forearm and measured rotation relative to the palm. Penalizing sustained outward displacement increased 25-second retention from 35.1% to 74.8% on fresh starts. The fraction of trials combining retention, supported rotation through 45°, and repeated command-linked finger exchanges rose from 10.1% to 36.1%. These results identify different requirements for target holding, forearm-assisted reorientation, and sustained in-hand manipulation, with substantial variation across training seeds on the latter task.
